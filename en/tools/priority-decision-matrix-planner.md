## Why use a Priority Decision Matrix Planner?
When several tasks feel urgent at once, **deciding what to start first can waste more time than the work itself**.
That usually happens because the list mixes together:

- work with an approaching deadline
- work with a large outcome impact
- quick tasks that can be finished soon
- work that still has significant uncertainty

Enter tasks or options and rate their **urgency, impact, effort, and confidence**. The planner then separates what to do now, what to handle this week, and what to review later in one ranked view.

## How to use it
1. Add up to five tasks or options.
2. Rate urgency, impact, effort, and confidence for each one.
3. Review the ranked result and its `Do now`, `This week`, or `Review later` label.
4. Copy the result into notes, meeting minutes, or a task manager.

Duplicate item names are flagged instead of being ranked twice. Names support up to 80 characters and context notes up to 120 characters. Entries and results stay in your browser.

## How the priority score works
The score uses `35% urgency + 40% impact + 15% low effort + 10% confidence`. Effort is inverted, so a smaller job receives a larger quick-win contribution. This is a practical task-ranking heuristic, not an objective measure of value.

- A score of 4.1 or higher, or both urgency and impact at 4+, is labeled `Do now`.
- A score of 3 or higher, or impact at 4+, is labeled `This week`.
- The remaining items are labeled `Review later`.

Ties are resolved by impact, urgency, and then input order. Real deadlines, dependencies, staffing, safety, and legal obligations should override the suggested order.

## Good use cases
### 1. Reordering today’s overloaded task list
When urgent and important work is mixed together, you can quickly decide what deserves attention first.

### 2. Handling several team requests at once
Comparing each request’s impact and actual effort makes the recommended order easier to explain.

### 3. Choosing which idea to test first
The ranking can surface a **small, quickly testable idea** instead of automatically favoring the largest project.

## FAQ
### What if I am not sure which rating to choose?
The ratings do not need to be perfect. Relative comparison is enough, as long as you apply the same standard to every item.

### What about high-effort work that still must be done?
It may appear lower in the ranking, so treat it as an exception when its impact or real deadline requires action. This planner supports a decision; it does not replace one.

### Can I use it for a study plan?
Yes. It also works for prioritizing assignments, review sessions, exam preparation, and application writing.

The Priority Decision Matrix Planner is a **planner-type tool** that helps you rank tasks by urgency, impact, effort, and confidence instead of relying on vague gut feeling alone.
